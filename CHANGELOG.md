# Changelog

Every release of `ashlar-tabular` that changes something you can see. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); before 1.0, a minor version may change the API.

## Unreleased

### Fixed

- A table with only categorical columns no longer raises on the default `num_embeddings` or on `numeric_scaling="quantile"`. Both have nothing to act on there and are skipped; the categorical columns are still embedded. Saving and loading such a model works too.

## 0.0.5 — 2026-11-03

### Added

- **Pooled groups, monotone features, and early-ending rows in a chain.**
  - `task="chain"` takes `random_effects`: every column with a location gets an offset of its own per group, with its own spread between groups, and a `categorical` column one per class after the first. All columns train together on one trunk, so a lender can be pooled across the cause, the time, and the loss of a loan in one fit.
  - `task="chain"` takes `monotone=`, on `gaussian`, `student_t`, `mixture`, count, `hurdle`, and two-class `categorical` columns. A column's own `{"monotone": {"rate": -1}}` flips a feature's direction for that column.
  - A chain's label may end early: a censored or interval cell before the last column is scored where every later cell of its row is unknown, such as a loan still running, with a censored time and no loss yet.
  - [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#outcomes-of-different-kinds-chained) has an example.
- **Forecast paths with covariates, as a table.**
  - New module `ashlar.horizon`. `horizon_frame(data, target=..., lookback=..., horizon=..., series=..., time=...)` turns a long table of many series into one row per series and forecast origin. Its features are the target's last `lookback` values, the same lags of each `past` column, each `known` column at every step ahead, and the series' `static` columns. Its outcome is the next `horizon` values.
  - Any `ASRRegressor` fits it. A joint head (`multivariate_gaussian`, `multivariate_mixture`, `multivariate_flow`) forecasts the whole path in one pass, with an exact log density and draws whose days move together.
  - `peak` and `stockout(stock)` are path functions for `calibrate(..., target=[...])`, `predict_interval`, and `predict_exceedance`. `scale="mean"` puts series of different sizes on one scale and `frame.unscale` puts draws back; `stride`, `start`, `end`, and `complete=False` choose the origins.
  - On Rossmann's daily sales 12 days ahead, the covariates cut the daily error by 64%. The joint normal's 80% interval for the 12-day total holds 76% of totals, where the same model with independent days holds 51%, and its CRPS for the total is 5.7% lower. It does little for the peak day, where `multivariate_flow` does best.
  - Chronos-2, zero-shot, matches the joint normal day by day, 467.6 against 477.6, but gives no paths. DeepAR, trained to convergence, scores 625 a day, and its total's CRPS is 3,881 against 2,623, though its peak day is the best calibrated of any model.
  - Calibrating on spring origins brought the total's 80% interval to 80% to 85% in summer, but left the peak's at 69% to 73%: split conformal needs calibration origins like the ones forecast.
  - `examples/benchmark_paths.py` runs it. [docs/forecast.md](docs/forecast.md#many-series-covariates-and-paths) is the guide, and [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#forecast-paths-with-covariates) has the tables.
  - The issue proposed replacing `ASRForecaster` and `ASRHistoryForecaster`. They stay as they are, and the table is the route for covariates, many series, and paths. ([#43](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/43))
- **Additive models of the whole distribution, a neural GAMLSS.**
  - `backbone="additive"` gives each column of `X` a small network of its own, and each pair named in `backbone_kwargs={"pairs": [...]}` one more. The networks meet only in the head, which is linear in them, so each of the head's outputs (a location, a raw scale, a log mean, a logit) is an intercept plus one shape per term.
  - Works on every built-in head but `multivariate_flow` and `chain`, so an additive model also gets the count and hurdle heads, mixtures and flows, observed labels, monotone constraints, entity effects, and `calibrate`.
  - `model.shape_functions(X)` reads the terms back per row, term, and output, with `importance` and `to_frame`, and keeps members apart in `member_values`. New module `ashlar.shapes`.
  - On hourly bike rentals, the additive negative binomial matches R's `gamlss` with penalized splines: CRPS 42.25 against 42.15, and 23.46 against 23.40 with the hour × working day pair. Each fit takes 8 to 10 seconds, against 2 to 45 minutes for `gamlss`.
  - On abalone rings, the additive `mixture` beats `gamlss`'s Box-Cox Cole-Green, CRPS 1.085 against 1.096. `gamlss`'s normal beats the additive `gaussian`, 1.115 against 1.156.
  - The residual network, with every interaction, scores 16.48 on bike rentals: what additivity costs there.
  - `examples/benchmark_additive.py` runs the comparison and draws `docs/images/additive-bike.png`. [docs/additive.md](docs/additive.md) is the guide, and [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#additive-models-against-rs-gamlss) has the tables.
  - The research models `ASRAdditive` and `ASRGatedAdditive` are marked experimental and point to `backbone="additive"`. ([#42](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/42))
- **Entity effects, pooled across groups.**
  - `random_effects={"store": "intercept"}` on `ASRRegressor` takes the grouping column out of the network. Each group gets a variational offset on the head's location, `N(0, tau²)` with `tau` learned. A numeric feature named among the terms gets a random slope.
  - `"embedding"` adds a pooled vector per group that the network reads, shrunk the same way, so a group can interact with the other features.
  - `{"terms": [...], "within": "class"}` nests a group in a broader one.
  - A seen group reads its posterior. An unseen or missing group reads the population's.
  - When training stops, `fit` sets every group's offsets to their posterior under the trained network: Laplace's method from the prior on all of the group's rows, exact on `gaussian`.
  - `with_context(X, y)` updates the offsets from a few rows of new groups without refitting.
  - `random_effects_` reports each spread and every group's posterior.
  - Works with `regression`, `gaussian`, `mixture`, `flow`, `student_t`, `quantile`, `quantile_spline`, `poisson`, `negative_binomial`, and `hurdle`. New module `ashlar.effects`; [docs/effects.md](docs/effects.md) has the detail.
  - On Rossmann's 1,115 stores, offsets plus embeddings score a CRPS of 0.096 on stores with 1 to 5 training rows. The ordinary embedding scores 0.120, GPBoost 0.125, and a linear mixed model 0.131.
  - An unseen store's 80% interval holds 75% of its rows, against 36% for the embedding, and five rows of a new store halve its CRPS.
  - The issue's amortized variant read a store's effect in context from its other rows through the set encoder. It lost to the exact update everywhere and was not built.
  - `examples/benchmark_effects.py` runs the comparison; [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#entity-effects) has the tables. ([#41](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/41))
- **Turning off the clip past the training range.** `numeric_clip` sizes the robust soft clip, 3 interquartile ranges by default, and `numeric_clip=None` turns every clip off: robust scaling becomes linear, and the quantile maps continue past their outermost knots on a straight line instead of stopping at the edge of the training rows, so a row past the range keeps moving the model. The schema and the saved model keep it. `examples/benchmark_uncertainty.py --stage extrapolate` tests it on three tables: on diamonds, whose price keeps rising with carat, the 80% interval past the range holds 27% of rows instead of 16%; on California, whose values are capped, and bike rentals, which fall in the hottest hours, it holds 9 to 18 points fewer. The clip stays the default, and the distance-aware members and engression objective the issue proposed were not built. [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#spread-past-the-training-range) has the detail. ([#34](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/34))
- **Monotone constraints, to stochastic dominance.** `monotone={"feature": 1 or -1}` keeps the named numeric features out of the trunk and adds them back through rising piecewise-linear shapes on the outputs that move every quantile one way, so `P(y > t | x)` cannot fall as the feature rises, for every `t`. For `regression`, `gaussian`, `student_t`, `poisson`, `negative_binomial`, and the new `quantile_spline` head, whose quantile function is piecewise linear in the logistic score, with an exact CDF and exponential tails, and whose spread can move with a constrained feature too. New module `ashlar.monotone` and distribution `ashlar.distributions.QuantileSpline`. `examples/benchmark_monotone.py` counts how often unconstrained heads break dominance in bonus-malus and vehicle power on freMTPL2: the claim count for 72% of policies and every cost head for all of them, none with the constraint, at 0.0013 to 0.012 nats. ([#40](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/40))
- **Observed labels on more heads.** Noisy labels on the `student_t`, `flow`, `histogram`, and `hurdle` heads, by Gauss-Hermite quadrature over the noise. Group totals on the `mixture`, `flow`, `histogram`, `student_t`, `negative_binomial`, and zero-inflated `poisson` heads, convolved on a grid. `head_kwargs={"cure": True}` on the `gaussian`, `mixture`, `flow`, and `student_t` heads adds a share of rows whose time never comes, a defective distribution (new `ashlar.distributions.Cured`). Classifiers take labels too: `observed.one_of` gives `ASRClassifier` a set of possible classes per row, and `observed.interval` gives `ASROrdinalClassifier` a range. [docs/observed.md](docs/observed.md) has recipes for cure models and competing risks, the second a chain of a cause and a censored time, and `examples/benchmark_observed_more.py` tests both and a joint mixture under regimes. ([#44](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/44))
- **Outcomes of different kinds, chained.** `task="chain"` with `head_kwargs={"columns": [...]}` predicts several columns together, each through a head of its own kind (`poisson`, `negative_binomial`, `hurdle`, `categorical`, `gaussian`, `mixture`, `flow`, `histogram`, `student_t`), each conditioned on the columns before it: `p(y₁ | x) p(y₂ | y₁, x) ⋯`. The joint log-likelihood is exact, a draw takes one column at a time, and `calibrate` and `predict_interval(..., target=...)` read functions of the columns, such as a policy's total cost, from the draws. A joint label may leave a categorical cell as a range of classes, which the likelihood sums over, and censor the last column. New module `ashlar.chain`. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#outcomes-of-different-kinds-chained) has the detail, and `examples/benchmark_chain.py` tests it on freMTPL2's claim counts and costs: 0.147 nats per policy better than independent heads, and tied with frequency and severity built by hand. ([#38](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/38))
- **Missing and noisy inputs widen the distribution.** `model.with_uncertain_inputs(draws=32, noise={"column": sd})` returns a view whose every prediction mixes the network's output over `draws` completions of each row: missing numeric cells drawn given the row's observed ones from a Gaussian copula of the numeric features fitted at `fit` (`input_model_`, saved with the model), and declared measurement errors drawn around the measured value. `predict_dist(X, input_draws=K, input_noise=...)` does it for one call. `input_log_density(X)` scores rows by `log p(x)`, and `fit_inputs(X)` refits the copula. New module `ashlar.inputs`. With 30% of California's test cells missing, the calibrated 80% interval covers 82.4% of rows, against 48.3% with the median fill. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#missing-and-noisy-inputs) has the detail. ([#35](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/35))
- **Decisions from the distribution.** New module `ashlar.decide`: the newsvendor's `critical_ratio`, `newsvendor_order`, and `newsvendor_cost`; `exceedance` and `cvar`, also of a weighted sum of a joint head's columns; `expected_utility` and `best_action` for any utility or cost by draws. `model.predict_order(X, under, over)` is the cost-optimal order, calibrated after `calibrate`, so its service level holds. Training on the decision's cost was benchmarked (`examples/benchmark_decide.py`) and not shipped: it did not beat the likelihood-trained mixture's plug-in order by the 1% the issue required. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#deciding-from-the-distribution) has the detail. ([#32](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/32))
- **Explanations of the distribution.** `model.explain(X, target)` attributes a quantile, the median, the mean, the predictive standard deviation, the width of a central interval, `P(y > threshold)`, or any `fn(dist)` to the features: integrated gradients for numeric features, occlusion against reference categories for categorical ones. It returns an `Explanation` with per-row `values`, `importance()`, and `to_frame()`. New module `ashlar.explain`. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#explaining-the-distribution) has the detail, and `examples/explain_bike.py` runs it on bike rentals. ([#37](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/37))
- **A traceable path from features to the distribution.** `model.encode(X)` host-encodes a frame, and `model.forward_dist(numeric_raw, categorical_onehot, ...)` returns the same `Distribution` as `predict_dist` from those arrays inside JAX, so `jax.grad` reaches the numeric features through imputation, scaling, the network, and the head. `model.dist_from_raw(raw)` is the public form of the step from raw head outputs to the distribution. ([#32](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/32))
- **Calibrated joint outputs.** `calibrate` works on `multivariate_gaussian`, `multivariate_mixture`, and `multivariate_flow`. It keeps a table of probability integral transforms for each column and for each function of the columns it is given with `target`: always their sum, and also a weight per column (that weighted sum) or a function defined with `def`. `predict_interval`, `predict_quantiles`, and `predict_exceedance` take the same `target`, so `predict_interval(X, 0.8, target="sum")` is a calibrated interval for the total. A function's table is saved under its name; pass the function again after `load`. Joint calibration needs rows whose cells are all exact. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#calibrating-the-columns-and-the-total) has the detail. ([#31](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/31))
- `predict_exceedance(X, threshold)`: P(y > threshold) per row for every head with a CDF, one probability per column on a joint head, or for a function of the columns with `target`. After `calibrate` the model's own `P(y ≤ threshold)` is replaced by the share of calibration rows whose transform was at most that much.
- `Distribution.project(weights)`: the exact distribution of a weighted sum of a joint outcome's columns, a normal under `LowRankMultivariateNormal` and `Independent` normals, and a mixture of normals under `Mixture` and `AffineTransformed` of them. The flow has no closed form and raises `NotImplementedError`.
- `examples/joint_demand.py calibrated`: the joint demand benchmark with the last 15% of each origin's training weeks held out to calibrate the total and each site.
- **Joint heads train on the energy and variogram scores.** `objective="energy"` trains a joint head on the energy score, and a dict blends proper scores with the log density, such as `{"nll": 1.0, "energy": 1.0}` or `{"nll": 1.0, "variogram": 1.0}`. The variogram score is refused on its own, since it cannot place the columns. Both are estimated without bias from 16 fresh reparameterized draws per row and step, and a mixture's from every component, weighted by the component probabilities. New `ashlar.objectives.energy_score`, `variogram_score`, `score_draws`, `score_weights`, and `make_joint_score_objective`. A callable objective with `stochastic = True` is passed a fresh `key=` on every training step. On the joint demand benchmark neither score did better than the log density, and the energy score alone made the total's interval narrower and less often right; `calibrate` is what fixes the total's coverage. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md#training-on-other-proper-scores) has the detail. ([#33](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/33))
- `examples/joint_demand.py scored`: the rank-2 normal and the flow trained on the energy score, alone and added to the log density, and the flow on the log density plus the variogram score, on the splits of the `calibrated` stage. Results in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#training-on-other-proper-scores).
- **Calibration by group.** `calibrate(X, y, groups=...)` calibrates each group of rows on its own (Mondrian conformal prediction), so an interval holds within every group with at least `min_group_rows` (100) calibration rows; smaller and unseen groups read the table of every row. `groups` is a column of `X`, read again at prediction, or one label per row, passed again as `groups=` to `predict_interval`, `predict_quantiles`, and `predict_exceedance`. `localize="members"` groups rows into up to five bins of how much the ensemble's members disagree, as a share of the predictive spread. Every head that calibrates takes them, the joint heads included. [docs/heads.md](docs/heads.md#calibrated-intervals) has the detail. ([#36](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/36))
- **Joint regions.** `predict_region(X, coverage)` gives a joint head's highest-density region per row as a log density threshold: `log_prob(X, Y) >= threshold` is inside. `calibrate` calibrates it on the model's probability of an outcome denser than the one seen, so the region holds `coverage` of new rows. New `ashlar.calibrate.density_pit`, `region_level`, and `region_threshold`.
- `examples/benchmark_uncertainty.py --stage groups` scores the 80% interval within groups of each calibration table before and after calibration per group, and `--stage localize` past the training range with `localize="members"`. `examples/joint_demand.py by_hour` calibrates the total per hour of day and scores the joint region. Results in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#coverage-within-groups): one calibration already holds within the groups of four of the five tables; a table per hour puts the electricity total near 80% at every hour; calibration takes the 80% joint region from 61% of hours to 79% for the joint normal and from 53% to 78% for the flow; and no calibration on rows in range, `localize="members"` included, holds the interval past the training range.
- `examples/benchmark_observed_real.py`: the labels that say what was observed, on real data. Survival on SUPPORT, METABRIC, and GBSG against XGBoost's AFT model, tuned on a grid, and a Cox model; drive failures with delayed entry on Backblaze's first quarter of 2024 against XGBoost AFT and pycox; demand from censored sales on FreshRetailNet-50K; and daily loads from meter readings. Results in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#observed-labels-on-real-data): fits to censored sales score within 0.005 nats of fits to the true demand, and their orders cost 4% more than the oracle's, against 12% for fits to sales; readings every 2 to 12 days teach the daily load nearly as well as the days, and weekly totals cannot place it within the week; the `mixture` head has a better likelihood than tuned AFT on two clinical tables of three and ties on the third; and on the drives, AFT fitted to the days watched with the age as a feature beats Ashlar's truncated likelihood. [docs/observed.md](docs/observed.md#survival-times) has a new section on survival times. ([#39](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/39))

### Changed

- **A new README, with a showcase page.** The README opens with a short description of the library, a figure of predicted delay distributions for 16 airline flights beside LightGBMLSS's, and a diagram of the five choices that define a model, with a paragraph on which other packages cover which features. Then come the install and quick start, a benchmark figure on eight large tables against LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, and LightGBMLSS, and a gallery of nine examples. Each tile links to its section of the new [docs/showcase.md](docs/showcase.md), which compares Ashlar with a tool commonly used for the same task (a gradient-boosted tree model, a mixed model, R's `gamlss`, XGBoost's survival model, or DeepAR) and gives the results, the cases where that tool does better, and the code. The showcase also has the benchmark tables and a section on the model object. `examples/readme/` redraws every figure from results committed under `examples/readme/artifacts/`. Both pages link by relative path; `examples/readme/absolute_urls.py` checks those links in CI and points the README's at the released commit for PyPI. The old forecasting and outcome-tree sections moved to [docs/forecast.md](docs/forecast.md#one-series-as-a-table-of-lags) and [docs/tabular.md](docs/tabular.md#tasks).

### Fixed

- The transformer and set backbones could read the wrong slice of their input when a table mixed numeric, one-hot, embedded, and set columns out of schema order. The core concatenates its fields grouped by kind, but `feature_chunks` listed them in schema order. Fields now follow the core's order, saved with the model as `field_order`, and a model saved without it loads with the old order. ([#42](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/42))
- The log density of `multivariate_gaussian`, and of each component of `multivariate_mixture`, lost its precision in float32 when a column's variance sat at its floor next to a covariance factor of ordinary size, as on a day the features make certain, such as a store known to be closed. The capacitance `I + WᵀD⁻¹W` could no longer hold its identity, so the density came out wrong or `NaN`, and a joint normal fitted on Rossmann's lags alone stopped on a loss that was not finite. The density now takes the QR of `[D^(-1/2) W; I]`, which never forms that matrix: on draws with variances at 1e-8, the old form was `NaN` for 22 of 300 and off by up to 1,300 times, and the new one is within 2% of float64 for all. It costs about 40% more to evaluate, a small part of a training step. ([#43](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/issues/43))

## 0.0.4 — 2026-10-31

### Added

- **Labels that say what was observed.** `ashlar.observed` builds a `y` that records what was seen about each value rather than the value itself: `exact`, `interval`, `censored` (right or left), `survival` (with delayed entry), `rounded`, `truncated`, `noisy`, and group totals (`total`, `grouped`). They compose, and `concatenate` mixes kinds in one table. Pass one as `y` to `fit`, `log_prob`, `score`, or `calibrate`, and the head trains on the probability it gives to the observation; `predict_dist` still describes the value itself. Every univariate head with a CDF takes them: `gaussian`, `mixture`, `flow`, `histogram`, `student_t`, the count heads, `hurdle`, and `normal`. Noisy labels need `gaussian`, `mixture`, or `normal`, and totals `gaussian`, `normal`, or `poisson`. A label is a NumPy structured array, so scikit-learn splits it. [docs/observed.md](docs/observed.md) has the detail. ([#29](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/29))
- `calibrate` takes interval and censored rows, drawing each row's PIT uniformly over what its label allows.
- Group totals train each group whole inside one minibatch and keep it on one side of an internal validation split. A split that cuts a group raises.
- `Distribution.log_cdf`, `log_survival`, `log_cdf_left`, `log_survival_left`, `log_interval`, and `cdf_interval` on every univariate distribution, computed in log space so they stay finite far into the tails, and `convolve_normal` on the normal, mixture, and affine distributions.
- `examples/benchmark_observed.py`: demand fitted from sales when a third of days sell out, and daily values fitted from weekly totals, each against the usual fits and an oracle. Its `figure` stage draws the README figure `docs/images/sold-out-demand.png`. Its results are in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#labels-that-say-what-was-observed).
- **Joint labels.** The joint heads take a label with a cell per target column, shape `(rows, columns)`, each cell exact, an interval, censored, or rounded: `exact`, `interval`, `censored`, `survival` (without delayed entry), and `rounded` accept tables, and `observed.stack` puts one-column labels side by side. A row scores the joint density of its exact cells times the probability of its coarse cells given them. `fit`, `log_prob`, and `score` take them; truncation, noise, and totals stay one-column. ([#30](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/30))
- `Distribution.log_box(lower, upper, points)` on `LowRankMultivariateNormal`, `AutoregressiveFlow` (one block), `Mixture`, `AffineTransformed`, and `Independent`. The joint normal runs the GHK simulator on its low-rank factors, one Kalman update per column, exact columns first and then the coarse ones from the least likely, so a row with at most one coarse column is closed form; the flow runs it in its own column order. Several coarse columns are integrated at quasi-random `box_points`, 32 per row in training and 256 when scoring. Checked against SciPy's multivariate normal CDF and against quadrature of the flow's density.
- **Joint mixture head.** `task="multivariate_mixture"` predicts a mixture of joint normals, five by default (`head_kwargs={"components": 5, "rank": 2}`), each with its own weight, location, and low-rank covariance per row, for dependence that comes in regimes. New `joint_mixture_distribution`. ([#30](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/30))
- `examples/benchmark_observed.py joint`: four products whose shelves empty together, the three joint heads fitted to the true demand, to sales, and to the censored joint label, against a censored mixture per product. The censored joint normal comes within 0.013 nats of the fit to the true demand. The per-product fits are 1.36 nats behind it and expect every shelf to empty on 0.7% of days, where it happened on 11.1%; the censored joint fits expect 9.1% to 12.1%. Its `joint_figure` stage draws `docs/images/joint-sold-out.png`, and the results are in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#several-products-that-sell-out-together).

### Fixed

- **The `flow` head gives each row its own spread and tails.** It was a spline on a fixed range with identity tails, so the spread past that range, and so every far quantile and tail probability, was the same for every row: on a lognormal target whose spread more than doubles across rows, it put 0.13% of every row's mass above a high threshold, where the truth is 1.8% on average and 4.4% in the riskiest tenth of rows. The head now ends in a shift and a scale per row, `y = shift + scale * spline(z)`, and gives 1.8% and 4.3%. Its output is two values wider per step. Models and forecasters saved with the old head load and predict as they did. ([#29](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/29))
- `jax.grad` through a Student-t's `cdf` or `quantile` in its degrees of freedom, or a negative binomial's `cdf` in its dispersion, raised, because JAX's `betainc` has no gradient in its shape arguments. Both now differentiate, by central differences in those arguments.

## 0.0.3 — 2026-10-31

### Added

- **One distribution object for every head.** `model.predict_dist(X)` returns an `ashlar.distributions.Distribution` in the units of `y` for every task that has a likelihood: the distribution heads, `normal`, the count heads, and, as a `Categorical`, classification, ordinal, and tree models. It has explicit `batch_shape` and `event_shape`, `log_prob`, `sample`, `mean`, `variance`, and for one target column `cdf` and `quantile`. It is an Equinox module, so it passes through `jax.jit`, `jax.vmap`, and `jax.grad`, and the draws of the normal, Student-t, and flow heads are reparameterized. `dist[rows]` slices it like an array. [docs/distributions.md](docs/distributions.md) has the detail. ([#23](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/23))
- `ASRForecaster.predict_dist` and `ASRHistoryForecaster.predict_dist` return the same objects, one marginal per horizon step.
- **Joint outputs.** `task="multivariate_gaussian"` takes `y` with one column per outcome and predicts their joint normal, with a covariance `W Wᵀ + diag(D)` of learned rank (`head_kwargs={"rank": 2}` by default) that changes from row to row. It trains on the joint log density. `predict_dist` gives the joint distribution in the units of `y`; `marginals()` gives each column on its own, and `Independent(dist.marginals())` the same columns with the dependence dropped. `predict` returns every column's mean and `predict_quantiles` each column's quantiles, `(rows, columns, levels)`. ([#24](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/24))
- **Joint flow.** `task="multivariate_flow"` takes the same `y` as `multivariate_gaussian` and predicts a conditional masked autoregressive flow over the columns: each column's conditional is a spline whose knots, shift, and scale come from a masked network reading the columns before it and the row. The joint can be skewed and its dependence nonlinear. Exact `log_prob`, reparameterized draws, and Monte Carlo moments; `head_kwargs={"hidden": 64, "depth": 1}`. New `AutoregressiveFlow` and `Empirical` distributions. ([#26](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/26))
- **Hurdle head.** `task="hurdle"` predicts the probability that `y` is exactly zero and, otherwise, a `lognormal` (default) or `gamma` amount (`head_kwargs={"severity": "gamma"}`), on the raw target. New `LogNormal`, `Gamma`, and `Hurdle` distributions. Calibration and quantiles handle the atom at zero. ([#24](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/24))
- `Distribution.cdf_left(y)`, `P(Y < y)`, so the randomized PIT works for any distribution with an atom.
- `examples/joint_demand.py`: eight electricity clients forecast jointly a day ahead and scored at 25 rolling origins against the same marginals made independent, independent per-client heads, and independent heads with a fixed residual correlation. Its results are in the README and in [docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md#joint-demand-across-locations). ([#25](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/25))

### Changed

Upgrading from 0.0.2: quantiles, intervals, calibration, and member uncertainty will differ slightly because they are now computed exactly rather than from draws, and code that checks the type of `predict_dist` on a `normal` model, or indexes its `(rows, 1)` moments, needs updating.

- `sample`, `log_prob`, `predict_quantiles`, `predict_interval`, and `calibrate` now read `predict_dist`, so they agree by construction.
- `predict_quantiles` and `predict_interval` are exact instead of read from 1,000 draws: closed form for the gaussian, flow, and histogram heads, and bisection on the CDF of the member mixture otherwise. They no longer depend on `n_samples` or `seed`, which are kept for custom distributions without a quantile.
- `calibrate` computes each held-out row's PIT from the CDF instead of ranking it among draws, randomized over the jump for a count head.
- `predict_uncertainty` reads each member's variance in closed form instead of from draws; `n_samples` and `seed` are unused.
- `predict_dist` on a `normal` model returns the new `Mixture` (or a `Normal` for `policy="mean_parameters"`) instead of `NormalMixture` and `MeanParameterNormal`. Those two classes still import. Its `mean()` and `variance()` are now `(rows,)` for one target column, not `(rows, 1)`.
- `log_prob` and `sample` work on classifiers too: a log probability of the observed label, and drawn labels.
- The calibrated-interval benchmark was rerun with exact quantiles; the `mixture` column moved by at most 0.9 points.

## 0.0.2 — 2026-10-30

### Added

- **Calibrated intervals.** `model.calibrate(X_holdout, y_holdout)` fits split-conformal calibration on rows the model did not train on. After that, `model.predict_interval(X, coverage=0.8)` gives intervals that cover the requested share of new, exchangeable rows. The method depends on the head:
  - Density heads use distributional conformal prediction on a randomized PIT.
  - The `quantile` head uses conformalized quantile regression.
  - Calibration is saved with the model and cleared by `fit`.
  - `calibrated=False` reads the raw head. ([#13](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/13))
- **Student-t head.** `task="student_t"` adds a heavy-tailed head with learned degrees of freedom, above 2. ([#14](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/14))
- **Count heads.**
  - `task="poisson"` and `task="negative_binomial"` model non-negative integer targets on the raw count. They give whole-number draws and quantiles, plus a log mass you can compare across models.
  - `head_kwargs={"zero_inflated": True}` adds a zero-inflation component.
  - Targets that are not counts are rejected. The forecasters do not take these heads. ([#14](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/14))
- **Member uncertainty.** `model.predict_uncertainty(X)` splits predictive uncertainty into `epistemic` (disagreement between ensemble members), `aleatoric` (the noise the head predicts) and `total`.
  - Classifiers and ordinal models report entropies instead, with mutual information as the epistemic part.
  - It needs an ensemble backbone such as `backbone="tabm"`. A one-member backbone reports zero epistemic uncertainty. ([#15](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/15))
- **Benchmarks** for all three features. They are in `examples/benchmark_uncertainty.py`, with results in `docs/benchmarks.md` under Calibrated intervals, Count heads and Member uncertainty. ([#17](https://github.com/maxmbay/ashlar/pull/17))

### Changed

- After `calibrate`, `predict_quantiles` returns recalibrated levels by default. On a model that was never calibrated it behaves as before.

### Notes

- The benchmarks turned up two calibration bugs, and both were fixed before release:
  - Calibration could not move a head whose PIT fell on the grid of its draws.
  - Calibrated intervals undercovered by about 0.7 points, because they read interpolated quantiles while the PIT ranked against order statistics.
- The member-uncertainty benchmark has two splits for California and one each for diamonds and bike rentals. Read it as indicative.
